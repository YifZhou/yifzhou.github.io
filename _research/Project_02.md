    <h1>How young planets grow</h1>
    <p class="lead">Young giant planets gain mass from the gas around them. We measure the light released by this process, called accretion, to study how planetary growth changes over time.</p>
  </header>

  <figure class="research-theme__figure">

  <div class="research-theme__sections">
    <section aria-labelledby="accretion-question">
      <h2 id="accretion-question">The question</h2>
      <p>How does gas reach a growing planet, and is that flow steady or variable?</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="accretion-observe">
      <h2 id="accretion-observe">What we observe</h2>
      <p>We use ultraviolet, optical, and infrared images and spectra to measure accretion emission. Careful subtraction of the host star's light lets us isolate the much fainter planet.</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="accretion-learned">
      <h2 id="accretion-learned">What we have learned</h2>
      <p>PDS 70 c became significantly brighter in hydrogen-alpha emission between our 2020 and 2024 Hubble observations. This line traces accretion, so the change suggests that the planet's growth is variable. <a href="{{ base_path }}/deep-dive/pds70c-variable-accretion/">Read the Deep Dive</a> or <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...980L..39Z/abstract">the paper on ADS</a>.</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="accretion-matters">
      <h2 id="accretion-matters">Why it matters</h2>
      <p>Variable accretion affects both how planets grow and when we can detect them. Repeated observations constrain the gas flow and help us interpret surveys that find few forming planets.</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="accretion-students">
      <h2 id="accretion-students">Opportunities for students</h2>
      <p>Students can reduce images, measure planetary brightness, compare observations from different dates, and test accretion models.</p>
    </section>
