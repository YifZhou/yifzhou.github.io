    <h1>Irradiated brown dwarfs</h1>
    <p class="lead">A brown dwarf in a close orbit around a white dwarf can keep one side facing its companion. We compare the heated dayside with the cooler nightside to study atmospheric chemistry and heat transport.</p>
  </header>

  <div class="research-theme__sections">
    <section aria-labelledby="irradiated-question">
      <h2 id="irradiated-question">The question</h2>
      <p>How much heat moves from day to night, and how does external heating change the atmosphere's chemistry?</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="irradiated-observe">
      <h2 id="irradiated-observe">What we observe</h2>
      <p>We measure spectra throughout the binary's orbit. As different hemispheres come into view, their changing emission lets us compare the day- and nightside atmospheres.</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="irradiated-learned">
      <h2 id="irradiated-learned">What we have learned</h2>
      <p>Broski-Laing et al. (2026) find inefficient day-to-night heat transport on ZTF0038B and an asymmetric nightside carbon dioxide absorption feature. The nightside emission also constrains the system's age. <a href="{{ base_path }}/deep-dive/ztf0038-day-and-night/">Read the Deep Dive</a> or <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260630112B/abstract">the preprint on ADS</a>.</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="irradiated-matters">
      <h2 id="irradiated-matters">Why it matters</h2>
      <p>These systems let us test how external heating competes with heat from a brown dwarf's interior. Comparisons with giant planets help us understand which atmospheric processes depend on rotation, gravity, and irradiation.</p>
    </section>
    <section aria-labelledby="irradiated-students">
      <h2 id="irradiated-students">Opportunities for students</h2>
      <p>Students can measure brightness changes over an orbit, compare molecular absorption across the atmosphere, and test models of temperature and composition.</p>
    </section>
